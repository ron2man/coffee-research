Your machine is almost certainly fine. On a {{product:barista-express}}, a fast shot with a needle that barely moves is exactly what the manufacturer's own booklet describes as an under-extracted shot. On a {{product:barista-pro}} the sign is early flow on the shot clock. Every cause the booklet lists for it is something you can change yourself. Work through the list in this order, one change per shot: basket, dose, shot mode, grind, inner burr, beans, routine, and temperature last. Most owners end up buying nothing.

## Read the shot, not the taste

This is the trap that sends people the wrong way. The manufacturer's Extraction Guide says a fast, under-extracted shot "tastes bitter/sharp, weak and watery". It says a slow, over-extracted one tastes "bitter and burnt". So "bitter" covers both. Only the summary table at the bottom of the page links under-extraction to "sour". If you can't tell sour from bitter, you're in good company (one Express owner on Lemmy said they had "misjudged sour as bitter" at first). Ignore the taste words and read the clock instead.

- **{{product:barista-express}}:** time the whole shot. The booklet's target is 25–30 seconds, and anything under 20 seconds is under-extracted. Its gauge zones are *Under*, *Within* and *Over Espresso Range*, with no bar values. The booklet gives two different flow-start figures (4–7 s on one page, 8–12 s on another), so go by total time, not flow start.
- **{{product:barista-pro}}:** there's no gauge. The LCD shot clock counts up once the shot starts. Flow starting at 8–12 seconds is right. Before 7 seconds means under-extracted. Nothing after 13 seconds means over-extracted.

If the shot is fast, the gauge is low or the flow starts early, the shot is under-extracted. The fix is a fuller basket and a finer grind, whatever word you'd use for the taste.

## The test to run tonight

Pull one shot set up like this:

1. Use the **single-wall 2-cup basket**.
2. Dose so that the tamper's **metal cap sits level with the basket rim after tamping**.
3. Pull a **manual shot**: press and hold 1 CUP or 2 CUP, release for full pressure, and press again to stop.

Note the total time and the gauge zone (Express) or the second the flow starts (Pro). Then let the tool pick your next change:

## The order, and why each step comes where it does

**1. The basket.** The booklet says dual-wall baskets "regulate the pressure and help to optimize the extraction regardless of the grind, dose, tamp pressure or freshness". That's handy for pre-ground coffee, but useless for dialing in, because it hides everything you change. The {{product:barista-pro}} ships with the single-wall 2-cup basket fitted. If you've been using a dual-wall basket, swap it out and pull a shot before you touch anything else.

**2. The dose.** Neither machine doses by weight. On the {{product:barista-express}}, GRIND AMOUNT is a dial: the booklet says to start at 3 o'clock and turn towards MORE or LESS until the tamper cap sits level after tamping. On the {{product:barista-pro}}, GRIND AMOUNT is a grind time, 9.5 s for a single and 13 s for a double by default. If the cap sits below the rim, turn GRIND AMOUNT up before you change the grind. If you already own a kitchen scale, weigh against the booklet's 16–19 g for the 2-cup basket. If you don't own one, the cap check costs nothing.

**3. The shot mode.** A single press of 1 CUP or 2 CUP stops at a preset volume, about 30 ml or 60 ml. The booklet itself says the amount in the cup varies with grind size and grind amount. So while you dial in, the button can hide what your change did. Pull manual shots until you're happy, then reprogram the button if you want.

**4. The grind.** Go one step finer per shot. On the Express, 1 is finest and 16 coarsest. On the Pro, 1 is finest and 30 coarsest, and setting 15 is the default. Check the tamper cap again after every grind change. This is the "finer made it worse" trap: because GRIND AMOUNT runs by time, the booklet warns that a finer grind in a single-wall basket may need a larger grind amount. Grind finer without raising the dose and you end up with a half-empty basket that still gushes. One Express owner said grinding finer "just slows down the shot, makes it even more sour". It got better once they changed more than the grind dial.

**5. The inner burr.** Only do this once the outer dial is at 1 and shots are still fast. The booklet presents the adjustable upper burr as a feature of both machines and gives an illustrated procedure. Empty the hopper, run the grinder empty, lift the burr out by its wire handle and move it one number finer. Do one number at a time, then retest. If you're at this point, use this tool first:

**6. The beans.** The booklet wants a "Roasted On" date (not a best-before) and beans used 5–20 days after it. The {{product:barista-pro}} booklet says stale coffee "may pour too quickly" and taste "bitter and watery". That's the same symptom you're chasing. Supermarket beans with no roast date can defeat every other step.

**7. The routine.** The booklet says a cold portafilter and basket can cut the extraction temperature "enough to significantly affect the quality" of the shot. It also says moisture in the basket encourages channeling. Run a short flow through the group, warm the portafilter and basket, wipe the basket dry, then dose and brew.

**8. Temperature, last.** It's the smallest lever, so it goes at the end. On the {{product:barista-express}}, use Advanced Temperature Mode: with the machine off, hold PROGRAM and press POWER. The booklet allows up to 4°F (about 2°C) either way, in 2°F (about 1°C) steps. Holding PROGRAM until it beeps three times resets the default temperature *and* your programmed shot volumes. On the {{product:barista-pro}}, use SHOT TEMP in the menu; the booklet doesn't give its range. One step, then retest.

The {{product:barista-express}} Impress doses differently (it has an assisted tamp), so steps 2 and 4 may not apply to it as written.

## Is it the machine?

Probably not, and the booklet backs that up. Its troubleshooting row for "Espresso runs out too quickly" lists three causes: coffee ground too coarsely, not enough coffee in the basket, and too light a tamp. Its "no crema" causes add stale beans, a blocked basket and the wrong basket. On either machine, the only row that tells you to call support is the thermal cut-out, where the machine switches off mid-use. Descaling doesn't belong on this list either: the booklet links scale build-up to reduced or dripping flow, not gushing. Advice online that quotes bar figures for the gauge doesn't come from the booklet, which names zones only.

One OzBargain thread shows how this usually goes. A new Express owner asked whether to return the machine because the gauge "does not move at all". They moved the grind to 1–2, filled the basket higher and tamped harder, and the needle moved. Their verdict was "I had no idea the Barista Express was so finicky about the dose", and they kept it. That's one owner, but it's the pattern the booklet's own tables predict.

What nobody can give you is a proper test for a real fault. The manufacturer documents no owner check that separates a pump, valve or gauge fault from a dial-in problem, and we won't invent one. So the rule is simple. If you've done all eight steps (single-wall basket, cap level, manual shot, finest outer setting, inner burr one step finer, fresh beans, preheated and dry) and the shot still runs fast, stop adjusting. Contact {{brand:barista-express}} support while the machine is under warranty, and give them the list of what you checked.

## Keep yours: what not to buy

Don't buy a new machine, a pressure-mod kit or a different basket; you already own the single-wall basket you need. A scale accurate to 0.1 g is handy but optional, because the tamper-cap check does the same job for dialing in. If the eight steps get your shot into range, you're done and you've spent nothing.

## When I'd choose differently

- **The inner burr is already at its finest and fresh beans still gush.** By then you've used every adjustment the grinder has, so the built-in grinder is your limit, not the machine. Keep the machine and look at a separate grinder (the picker below helps).
- **You mostly drink very light roasts.** My expectation is that you'll hit the built-in grinder's limit sooner than owners on medium roasts. Run the same steps, but don't be surprised if you end up at the grinder picker.
- **Your shots drip or run past 35 seconds.** That's the opposite problem. The booklet's fix is a coarser grind and a smaller grind amount, so go one step coarser at a time.

## What to do next

If the steps work, buy nothing and stop changing things. If they don't, and the machine is under warranty, contact support with your checklist before you spend anything.
