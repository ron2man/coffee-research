Leave the grind dial alone for now. If your {{product:barista-pro}} starts, runs a few seconds, beeps and stops, first run hot water with no portafilter fitted. If that cuts out too, the coffee isn't the problem. Work through four free checks on the water side, in order: the tank, the water filter, a descale and a settings reset. If it still beeps and stops after all four, don't open the machine and don't buy parts. Contact {{brand:barista-pro}} support while you're under warranty. If you aren't, get a written quote before you decide anything.

The troubleshooter below asks what you've already tried and stops at your next step. The rest of the page explains why each check is in its place.

## The test that splits the problem in two

It takes about a minute. Take the portafilter out, put a jug under the hot-water outlet and turn the dial to hot water. The manual says hot water stops by itself after 60 seconds, so a full minute of flow is normal. A few seconds and a beep is not the timeout. Next, put a cup under the empty group head and press 1 CUP. The manual has you do this anyway before every shot to preheat the group.

- **Both run normally, and only real shots cut out.** The water path is fine and the puck is choking the flow. For no flow, the manual lists a grind that's too fine, too much coffee, tamping too hard and a blocked basket. Go a few steps coarser, check your dose, clear the basket holes with the cleaning-tool pin, then follow the {{guide:barista-express-pro-sour-bitter-what-to-change-first|dial-in guide}}.
- **Hot water or 1 CUP also stops after a few seconds.** Nothing about the coffee causes that, so stop adjusting grind, dose and tamp. Go to the checks below.

If steam stops the same way, that points the same direction. A cut-out that hits shots, hot water and steam alike is what owners describe again and again.

## Four free checks, in this order

**1. The tank.** Push it down until it locks and fill it well above MIN. The manual gives an unseated or empty tank as a cause of no water and no steam, and says a tank below MIN can make the pump run loudly with nothing coming out. It's the cheapest check, so do it first. Then run the hot-water test again.

**2. The water filter.** Lift the filter out of the tank and run the hot-water test without it. If the cut-out stops, the filter was the problem, which is common right after fitting a new one. The manual says to soak a new filter in cold water for 5 minutes before you fit it. Replace it on schedule from then on: one edition of the manual says every 2 months, the other every 3, and both say more often in hard-water areas.

**3. A descale.** For no flow and for no steam, the manual's fix is a descale, and it says mineral build-up reduces brewing flow. Take the filter out, choose DESCALE from the menu and let all three stages run, then the rinse. Our {{guide:barista-express-pro-clean-descale-routine|clean-or-descale guide}} goes through it step by step.

**4. RESET DEFAULTS.** Press MENU, turn to RESET DEFAULTS, and the machine beeps and goes back to READY. To be honest, this only resets your grind amount, filter size, shot temperature and shot volumes. The manual never offers it as a fault fix. It's free and does no harm, so try it before you call support, but don't expect much from it.

One case sits apart from the rest. If the machine is on but won't do anything at all, the manual calls that the safety thermal cut-out. Switch it off, unplug it, let it cool for 30–60 minutes, and call support if it happens again. In the whole troubleshooting table, that's the only row that sends you to support.

## Still beeping? This is where you stop

The manual has no entry for a cycle that runs a few seconds, beeps and stops, and no list of beep codes. Beyond the four checks, everything you'll read online comes from owners and repairers, not the manufacturer, so weigh it that way. Owners on a forum and on iFixit describe this exact fault on shots, hot water and steam, sometimes intermittent and getting more frequent. An independent repair shop says that on this model a stop at about five seconds is usually the flow meter or its wiring, not an air lock. The flow meter is a small turbine the machine counts to confirm water is moving. That's one repairer's view, but it fits the owner reports, and you can't fix either part from outside the case.

So don't open the machine. The warranty covers repair or replacement of a machine that's defective in materials, workmanship or function. It excludes repairs by unauthorised parties and damage from not following the care instructions, so taking the case off yourself can cost you the claim.

**If you're under warranty, or not sure:**

1. Contact {{brand:barista-pro}} support through their support page, not the shop you bought it from.
2. Find the model number or batch code on the sticker under or on the back of the machine.
3. Have your receipt or a copy ready.
4. Tell them what you've already done: the hot-water test, the tank, running without the filter, the descale and the reset. That saves you a round of the same suggestions.

If the claim qualifies, they arrange shipping and the repair. A covered repair costs you nothing, which is why keeping the machine is the default here.

**If you're out of warranty,** get a written quote and ask for a genuine flow meter. The repairer warns against cheap generic or "compatible" copies. Then set the quote against what a replacement machine would cost you. Don't assume a repair is the end of it, either. One owner had a flow-control part replaced and the same fault came back about seven months later. I'd repair if the quote is a modest share of a new machine's price and the rest of the machine is in good shape. I'd replace if the quote is high, because that one report shows a repair may not last. I can't give you a number: I found no reliable repair costs for either market.

## What you'll end up buying (probably nothing)

If running without the filter fixed it, the only purchase is a replacement water filter. Soak it for 5 minutes before you fit it. If the descale fixed it, keep the machine and descale whenever the alert shows. If the tank or the reset fixed it, buy nothing. None of these outcomes needs a new machine, and a warranty claim doesn't either. A new machine only comes up if you're out of warranty and the quote is high.

## When I'd choose differently

**Your machine is brand new and still inside the return window.** Do the tank and filter checks because they take five minutes, and if it still cuts out, take it back or exchange it. A new machine that won't pour isn't yours to troubleshoot.

**Only real shots stop, and hot water runs the full minute.** None of the fault advice above applies to you. It's a dial-in problem, and the {{guide:barista-express-pro-sour-bitter-what-to-change-first|dial-in guide}} is the right page.

**You've already had this repaired once and it's back.** I'd lean toward replacing over a second paid repair, unless the first repair is still covered by the repairer's own guarantee. Then go back to them first.
