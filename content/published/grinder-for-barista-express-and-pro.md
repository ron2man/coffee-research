If the built-in grinder on your {{product:barista-express}} or {{product:barista-pro}} seems to have given up, don't buy anything yet. Four free checks come first, and a new upper burr is the cheapest fix after that. If the grinder still can't keep up, skip {{product:breville-smart-grinder-pro}} and decide on one question: should the new grinder outlive this machine? If yes, get {{product:turin-df54}}. If you're keeping this machine and want something simple, get {{product:baratza-encore-esp}}.

## The four free checks

The manual's troubleshooting table lists three causes for "espresso runs out too quickly": the grind is too coarse, there's too little coffee in the basket, or the tamp is too light. The burrs aren't on that list. So go through these in order and pull a shot after each one:

1. **Beans.** Look for a roasted-on date on the bag. The manufacturer says to use beans 5–20 days after roasting and warns that stale coffee pours too quickly and tastes bitter and watery. A bag with no roasted-on date can't be checked against that window, so use one that has a date for this test.
2. **Dose and basket.** Use a single-wall basket, not the dual-wall one meant for pre-ground coffee. Dose until the tamper's metal cap sits level with the basket rim after tamping.
3. **Clean the burrs.** Run the grinder empty, lift out the top burr, and brush both burrs and the chute as the booklet describes. The manual says this removes oil build-up that can impede performance.
4. **Step the upper burr finer.** The adjustable upper burr is a documented owner feature on both machines, meant for coffees that need a wider grind range. Move it one number finer, put it back and lock it, then pull a shot. Make one adjustment at a time, as the booklet says, and repeat until shots run in range.

## Already at the finest upper burr? Price a new burr before a new grinder

The smallest purchase that can bring a tired built-in grinder back is a replacement upper burr. There are two versions, and which one you need depends on the batch date on the sticker under the machine. A parts seller lists SP0027464 for machines with a PDC batch date of 1926 (2019, week 26) or later, and SP0001801 for older ones. Both listings name the Express and the Pro. That's one seller's listing, so confirm the part with the manufacturer or a local parts seller before you order.

If you own a Barista Express Impress, the free checks still apply, but we haven't confirmed which burr fits it. Ask the manufacturer's support, and have the model and batch date from the sticker to hand.

## Why not the manufacturer's own grinder

{{product:breville-smart-grinder-pro}} looks like the obvious pick: it's the same brand, and it comes with a cradle for 50–54 mm portafilters. For an Express owner it's a sideways move. CoffeeGeek's 2023 review says it uses a 40 mm conical burr group, "the same one that was in the Barista Express". A 2016 retailer comparison from Seattle Coffee Gear found the same thing: the Express burrs were conical stainless steel "like the Smart Grinder Pro". The CoffeeGeek reviewer also calls it "a clumpy, clumpy grinder for espresso" that retains a lot of grounds. On an Australian forum, one owner told an Express owner it "won't be an upgrade" from the built-in grinder, and others in the thread suggested a DF54 instead. That's one person's opinion, but it matches the review.

The Pro is a different case. The manufacturer says the Pro's built-in grinder uses Baratza burrs, and no source compares those with the Smart Grinder Pro's. So the "same burrs" argument doesn't hold for the Pro. The reviewer's complaints about clumping and retention still apply.

## Which grinder, then: the rule

Once you've done the free checks and dealt with the upper burr, one question settles it: **should this grinder outlive the machine?**

**Yes, or you mostly drink straight espresso and don't mind weighing doses: {{product:turin-df54}}.** It's a single-dose grinder with 54 mm stainless flat burrs and stepless adjustment, so you aren't stuck between clicks. In Coffeeblog's review, retention was 0.1 g or less from the third use. That's the reviewer's measurement, on one sample. The routine is to weigh the beans, grind into the dosing cup, then tip the coffee into the portafilter. A reader commenting on the review says the cradle is made for 58 mm portafilters, and that a 54 mm one can be pushed in and held. If it doesn't hold yours, grinding into the cup and transferring works on any machine, and the cradle will suit a 58 mm machine if that's your next step. UK buyers should buy from a UK retailer: Coffeeblog warns that overseas sellers may ship an EU plug with no UK warranty, and the courier may add import fees.

**No, you're keeping this machine, you mostly make milk drinks and you want to press a button: {{product:baratza-encore-esp}}.** It has 40 mm steel conical burrs on a quick-release mount, and settings 1–20 are its espresso range. Baratza lists a dosing cup intended for 54 mm portafilters, and grinding into a cup and transferring works either way. I'll be straight about this one: on paper it's another 40 mm conical grinder, and nobody has published a head-to-head test against the built-in grinder. Buy it because your built-in grinder is failing and you want a dependable separate grinder, not because it's proven to grind better.

## When I'd choose differently

- **The free checks worked.** Stop here. A new grinder won't improve on fresh beans and a full dose.
- **You plan to upgrade the machine, but weighing beans every morning would put you off espresso.** Get {{product:baratza-encore-esp}} anyway. A grinder you'll actually use beats a better one you resent.
- **Your built-in grinder stalls or cuts out rather than just grinding too coarse.** Ask the manufacturer about repair before you buy, especially if the machine might still be under warranty. Then follow the rule above.
- **You own a Barista Express Impress.** Do the checks, but don't rely on what this guide says about the Express's burrs. We haven't researched the Impress grinder.

## What to do, in order

1. Do the four free checks. If shots come back into range, buy nothing.
2. Already at the finest upper burr and still fast? Read the batch date under the machine, confirm the matching upper burr with the manufacturer or a parts seller, and replace that one part.
3. Still not right, or the grinder is failing? Choose by the rule: {{product:turin-df54}} if the grinder should outlive the machine, {{product:baratza-encore-esp}} if you're keeping this machine and want simple.
4. Whichever you pick, skip {{product:breville-smart-grinder-pro}} if you own an Express.

## How this guide was put together

We read the manufacturer's US instruction booklets for both machines page by page. We also read the US and UK product pages for the machines, the Smart Grinder Pro and the Encore ESP. We compared those with CoffeeGeek's Smart Grinder Pro review, Seattle Coffee Gear's 2016 comparison, Coffeeblog's DF54 review and its reader comments, a US retailer's DF54 page, an Australian parts seller's upper-burr listings and two OzBargain owner threads. We also read the top-ranking grinder guides for these machines to see what they leave out.

We did not test any of these grinders or machines. No published test compares the Encore ESP or the DF54 with the built-in grinder on the same machine. The DF54 cradle fit comes from a single reader comment, and we couldn't reach the DF54 manufacturer's own spec page. Nothing here covers the Barista Express Impress.
