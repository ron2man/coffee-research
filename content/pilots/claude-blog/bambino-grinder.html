Which grinder belongs beside your Breville Bambino?

Before you pick a grinder, pop the basket out of your Bambino. That little steel cup can settle the cheapest version of this question. If you buy your coffee already ground, use the dual-wall basket and like the espresso it makes, you can leave the space beside your machine empty.

The other basket is where a grinder earns its counter space. Breville supplies single-wall and dual-wall baskets with the US Bambino and Bambino Plus. It recommends the single-wall basket for freshly ground beans. If your machine came secondhand, check what you actually have in the drawer. Give the grinder you own two shots

Use one bag of beans, the single-wall basket and the same dose. Pull a shot. If the coffee races through, turn the grinder one step finer and try again. If you can bring the flow back to where you like it and find that setting again tomorrow, I’d spend the money on the next bag of beans.

Pay attention if one setting repeatedly gives you a fast pour and its neighbour barely lets coffee through. You already know the adjustment you want to make. Your fingers want to leave the dial halfway between those two settings. That’s a much better shopping brief than “I need a better grinder.” Breville’s guide to grind and extraction

When a numbered dial is a pleasure

For a first electric grinder, I’d put the Baratza Encore ESP beside the Bambino. Imagine yesterday’s espresso was lovely, but this morning you went one step finer and regretted it. Turn the hopper back one click. Being able to retrace a good shot is a fine reason to choose a numbered dial.

Baratza reserves settings 1 through 20 for espresso. The grounds land in a dosing cup sized for a 54 mm portafilter, so you can transfer them straight into the Bambino basket. I’d pick this setup for someone who tends to finish one bag before opening another and wants to get on with making coffee. Encore ESP specifications

When you already know you want half a click

If that gap between settings is the reason you’re shopping, I’d choose the Turin DF54. Its stepless dial lets you make the small move the numbered grinder couldn’t. The trade happens before the shot: you weigh out the beans for each drink and feed that dose into the grinder. Picture doing that again when someone asks for a second cup. If the scale already lives beside your machine, the routine may suit you beautifully.

Check the burr choice before ordering. The US DF54 v4.1 listing offers espresso and brew burrs. For this Bambino setup, I’d order the espresso version. DF54 versions and burr options

There’s also the 1Zpresso J-Ultra if you’d enjoy grinding by hand. Its external dial gives you fine control, but you supply the effort for each dose. The question I’d ask is simple: when you’ve just made your own espresso and somebody else wants one, will you be happy to pick up the handle again? J-Ultra adjustment guide
