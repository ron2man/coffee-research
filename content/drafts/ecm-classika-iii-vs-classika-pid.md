# ECM Classika III vs Classika PID: Is the faster warm-up worth paying for?

I’d buy the **Classika III** if the two machines were close in price. I’d take a **meaningfully cheaper, well-kept Classika PID** if I could switch it on before making breakfast and have it ready by the time I wanted coffee. The III is worth paying extra for if you regularly switch on the machine when you want an espresso and a shorter wait would let you make it sooner or fit it in before leaving.

## How much difference would a faster warm-up make?

ECM advertises **6.5 minutes** to brew readiness for the Classika III and **11 minutes** for the Classika PID it currently lists. The III uses heating cartridges built into the brew group. Both machines list a Fast Heat-Up mode. How much sooner you’d pull your first shot under the same conditions is still unknown. [Classika III catalogue](https://www.ecm.de/en/products/classika-iii/) · [Classika PID catalogue](https://www.ecm.de/en/products/classika-pid/)

The current PID manual tells you to flush when the display shows `FLU` and to brew once it shows `rdY/Go`. If warm-up is the reason you’d pay more, ask the seller to show you the machine from a cold switch-on through to the point where you can pull a shot, including any flush. For a used PID, ask which manual applies before counting on that sequence or the 11-minute figure. [Classika PID manual, §6.1](https://www.ecm.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BA_Classika_-PID_2412-web.pdf#page=26)

## How can I tell which Classika a seller is offering?

ECM calls the Classika III the successor to the Classika PID. If the listing just says “Classika”, ask for the full model and a photo of the controls or rating plate. ECM’s English catalogue lists the III as **CM100024-00**, with an OLED display and a dial you turn to browse settings and press to select. It lists the PID as **CM100003-00**. For a UK purchase, confirm the voltage, frequency and plug on the exact unit. If buying used, ask about service history and have the seller show that the machine brews and steams. ECM’s Classika PID manual lists the UK version at 230 V, 50 Hz. [ECM successor announcement](https://www.ecm.de/en/ecm-classika-iii-single-boiler-espresso-machine/) · [Classika III catalogue](https://www.ecm.de/en/products/classika-iii/) · [Classika PID catalogue](https://www.ecm.de/en/products/classika-pid/) · [Classika PID manual, §3.2](https://www.ecm.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BA_Classika_-PID_2412-web.pdf#page=24)

## Will the Classika III make back-to-back milk drinks easier?

Both machines use one boiler for brewing and steaming, so plan on switching temperatures between those jobs. ECM lists a 0.5 L boiler on the III and a 0.75 L boiler on the PID. If you mostly make milk drinks, ask for a demonstration from the first shot through steaming and back to the next shot. The cold-start figures don’t tell you how long that sequence takes.
