Which grinder should you buy for a Breville Bambino?

Get the Baratza Encore ESP Pro (about $300). It’s the grinder I’d point most Bambino owners to, and the reason is one word: stepless.

The Bambino is a great little machine, but it gives you almost nothing to adjust. You can’t tweak pressure or shot profile. The grind is the only dial you have. On a grinder with numbered steps, you can find yourself stuck between “gushes in 15 seconds” and “barely drips,” with nothing in between. A stepless collar lets you creep up on the right shot. The Pro is the first Encore that has one, plus a timer and a single-dose mode.

When I’d buy something else

You’ll use the dual-wall basket. Breville’s manual points pre-ground coffee to that basket, and it forgives a lot. If that’s you, save $100 and get the original Encore ESP. The Pro’s precision would be wasted.

You buy a new bag every week. Hopper grinders hold a little of the last coffee in the chute. If you swap beans constantly and don’t mind weighing every dose, the Turin DF54 is built for exactly that. It’s fiddlier, and it’s an espresso grinder first.

Already own a grinder? Try this first

Use the single-wall basket and the same dose every time. Pull a shot, move one setting finer, pull another, and time both to the same output weight. If one step takes you from rushing to choking, your grinder is the problem and the Pro is your fix. If one step changes things sensibly, keep what you have. The shot is being limited by your puck prep, or by the machine. Entry-level machines vary a lot from day to day, and no grinder fixes that.

One last thing: don’t overspend

A reviewer for Tom’s Guide ran two blind taste tests this year, and the cheaper setup won both, including the Encore ESP Pro over a $1,500 grinder. It’s one person’s test, not a lab result, but it matches the general advice: at Bambino level, a well-matched $300 grinder isn’t what holds you back.
